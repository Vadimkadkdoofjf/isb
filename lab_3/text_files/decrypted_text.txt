Modern neuroscience research demonstrates that the brain exhibits remarkable plasticity—the ability to reorganize synaptic connections in response to learning or injury. Key mechanisms underlying this process include long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), which depend on the activity of NMDA receptors.

Experiments on rodents have shown that enriched environments enhance neurogenesis in the hippocampus, improving cognitive performance. Additionally, synaptic pruning refines neural networks during development, optimizing information processing. Advances in neuroimaging, such as fMRI, allow scientists to observe these dynamic changes in vivo, providing insights into brain adaptation.

Understanding neuroplasticity has implications for treating neurological disorders, including stroke rehabilitation and memory-related diseases. Future research may uncover targeted therapies to harness plasticity for neural repair.
